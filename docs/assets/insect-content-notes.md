# Insect collection content and artwork

The child-facing collection contains 31 creatures: the original 30 insect groups drawn from garden, pond, household, and woodland encounters in Ireland, the Netherlands, and Taiwan, plus the user-requested tarantula moved from Animals. Tarantula is an arachnid; its placement is a game grouping, not a biological classification or a claim of regional occurrence. It is not a claim that each group or illustrated species is common in every country. Stick insects and stag beetles are retained from the original proposal as discovery entries. Fireflies are seasonal woodland encounters.

`src/data/insectProfiles.json` records a representative species and adult stage/sex where that matters. The original ant, bee, and butterfly images are deliberately stylised game illustrations; colours are not a field-identification guide. Four short clues teach HOME, FOOD, LOOKS, and SPECIAL. LOOKS usually uses a magnified detail of the custom portrait; tarantula shows its whole portrait so all eight walking legs can be counted. It does not have a bear alternative.

Only SPECIAL changes images: the insect performs the action in the custom illustration; the pink princess bear performs the matching action in the alternative. The existing bear bank is reused where appropriate, with additional bear illustrations for new abilities. The bear's magical actions are visual analogies, not biological claims about bears.

All 73 newly supplied collection images have genuine transparent backgrounds. After generated transparency edits returned painted backgrounds, the user authorised local background removal. The preparation script removes the white matte from the original illustrations, preserves detached action props, softens vignette edges, and saves real alpha in WebP. The new tarantula portrait and shelter-lining bear use semantic masks to remove their painted checkerboard backgrounds. Small illustrated action settings remain where they explain the ability. Alpha channels and composites on pink cards are checked; a white rectangle or painted checkerboard is not transparency.

## Fact checks that affect the cards

- [Maryland Zoo: Mexican red-kneed tarantula](https://www.marylandzoo.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Mexican-Red-kneed-Tarantula-Species-Profile_June-2024.pdf) and [San Diego Zoo: tarantula](https://animals.sandiegozoo.org/animals/tarantula) support eight hairy legs, insect prey, and silk-lined burrows. Its four clues are Burrow, Insects, Eight hairy legs, and Make silk. The custom Special art shows silk from the rear spinnerets lining a shelter; the princess bear stretches silk onto a matching shelter rim.
- [Cornell: adult European crane flies do not feed](https://turf.cals.cornell.edu/pests-and-weeds/insect-management/). The FOOD card therefore says 'Adults do not eat'; grass roots would describe the larva instead.
- [Taiwan East Rift Valley National Scenic Area: adult Taiwanese fireflies drink dew and nectar](https://www.erv-nsa.gov.tw/file/2013/). The selected black-winged firefly has a dew card, not its larval snail diet.
- [Cornell: adult common green lacewings eat nectar, honeydew and pollen](https://biocontrol.entomology.cornell.edu/predators/Chrysoperla.php). Aphids would describe their predatory larvae. Its SPECIAL card illustrates eggs on stalks.
- [Taiwan Biodiversity Network: Phraortes elongatus](https://www.tbn.org.tw/taxa/74518395-1d8a-4143-9aca-7734138d3032) includes the former name Phraortes illepidus. The profile uses the accepted name.
- [Taipei Zoo: rhinoceros beetles](https://www.zoo.gov.taipei/News_Content.aspx?n=BD065B2FA7782989&s=5BAE3FE572AA2B0F&sms=72544237BBE4C5F6) supports tree-sap feeding, summer activity, and a horn on males. The portrait represents a male. Stag-beetle lifting is performed with jaws, not a horn.
- [Wildlife Trusts: common pond skater](https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/invertebrates/bugs/common-pond-skater) supports the pond-surface habitat and skating behaviour.
- [WWT: diving beetles](https://www.wwt.org.uk/discover-wetlands/wetland-wildlife/meet-the-family/underwater-invertebrates-found-in-uk-wetlands) supports aquatic prey and swimming.
- [National Geographic Kids: ladybirds](https://kids.nationalgeographic.com/animals/invertebrates/facts/ladybug) supports aphids, spots, and playing dead.

The mosquito represents an adult male, so its nectar diet and feathery antennae do not describe blood-feeding females. The earwig represents a female guarding eggs. Cricket and cicada song cards represent males. Habitat labels describe places to find the insect, not necessarily its nest. Foods are examples, not exhaustive diets.

## Asset provenance

New raster artwork was produced with the built-in image generation tool. Prompts are in `insect-image-prompts.json`, with additional food and anatomy refinements in the neighbouring insect prompt files; selected generation output paths are in `insect-image-outputs.json`. Source originals remain in the Codex generated-images directory. `scripts/prepare-insect-images.py` removes the original white backgrounds and transcodes copies to WebP at up to 448 pixels for portraits and insect abilities, or 384 pixels for food and bear cards, at quality 75 with 32 alpha levels, for `src/assets/insects/`. It creates temporary contact sheets for visual inspection and does not alter the source originals.

To reproduce preparation, install `Pillow`, `numpy`, `scipy`, and `rembg[cpu]` in a separate Python environment, then run `python scripts/prepare-insect-images.py`. The script downloads U2Net on first use and caches its masks under `tmp/`. The model identifies enclosed background gaps only: using its entire foreground mask would lose meaningful musical notes and props. Edge colour is unmatted locally to avoid white fringes. The preparation dependencies and model are not part of the app runtime.

Shared Home/Food illustrations and the original ant, bee, butterfly, and existing princess-bear abilities remain in their existing asset folders. No animal artwork is deleted or overwritten.

The tarantula addition has three new built-in generated illustrations: `portraits/tarantula.webp`, `abilities/tarantula.webp`, and `generic/line-shelter.webp`. Prompts and anatomy corrections are recorded in `tarantula-image-prompts.json`; selected originals and per-image export settings are recorded in the main output manifest. Run the preparation script with `--ids portraits/tarantula abilities/tarantula generic/line-shelter` to reproduce only these assets. The original animal tarantula artwork is retained for other app activities.

To accommodate the addition within the existing asset budget, five large Special illustrations (grasshopper, bumblebee, cicada, lacewing, and dragonfly) use smaller exports from the same original artwork. Their size and quality overrides are recorded in the manifest; all retain genuine alpha and resolution above their 174-pixel card display size. The new tarantula portrait is 384 pixels; its Special pair is 320 pixels.
